# Research brief — 19 November 2026

## Topic: the photic sneeze reflex
Bright light can trigger sneezing in susceptible people. The phenomenon is also called photic sneezing or ACHOO syndrome.

## Established evidence and limits
- A 2025 open-access mini-review estimates the reflex affects approximately 25% of people, while noting variation in prevalence and study methods.
- The exact neural mechanism is not established. Proposed explanations include optic–trigeminal interaction, parasympathetic hypersensitivity and parasympathetic generalization; none has been conclusively validated.
- A 2010 EEG study found enhanced visual-cortex responses in photic sneezers. Nose-prickling was associated with activity in the insula and secondary somatosensory cortex. These findings do not prove a single causal route.
- A 2019 genome-wide association study of 3,417 Chinese participants (874 self-reported PSR-positive; 25.6%) identified associated variants in multiple regions. The trait appears polygenic; no single “sun-sneeze gene” explains it.

## Fact-check guardrails
- Do not state that the optic nerve directly misfires into the trigeminal nerve; that is one proposed explanation, not proven.
- Do not claim a single dominant “ACHOO gene”. Genetic studies show associations across variants, not a deterministic single gene.
- Do not specify a wavelength or exact brightness threshold; stimulus parameters remain poorly characterized.
- EEG group differences and correlated activation do not establish the causal pathway.
