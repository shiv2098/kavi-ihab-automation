Ever step out of a dark room into bright sunlight and suddenly sneeze? That reaction has a name: the photic sneeze reflex, sometimes nicknamed ACHOO syndrome. It affects about one person in four. The trigger appears to be a sudden jump in brightness, although the exact light conditions are still being studied. One leading idea is that strong visual signals interact with sensory pathways involved in the sneeze response. An EEG study found stronger visual-cortex responses in people who experience the reflex, along with activity in brain regions linked to body sensation. Genetics may contribute too: a study of 3,417 Chinese participants found several DNA variants associated with it. But those variants do not reveal the full mechanism. So the sun-sneeze is real; scientists are still mapping the wiring.
