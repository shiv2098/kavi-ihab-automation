## Description
In Mexico’s sulfur springs, tiny sulfur mollies create a wave-like ripple when birds attack. Each fish dives and nearby fish copy the movement, passing a defensive pattern across the shoal. Experiments found that the displays made birds wait longer between attacks. A 2026 field study shows the predators adapt too: kingfishers often target the shoal’s edges, where weaker waves are triggered. The exact sensory reason the waves work is still being investigated.

Research: Doran et al., *Current Biology* (2021), https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cub.2021.11.068; Pacher et al., *Proceedings of the Royal Society B* (2026), https://doi.org/10.1098/rspb.2026.0566.
