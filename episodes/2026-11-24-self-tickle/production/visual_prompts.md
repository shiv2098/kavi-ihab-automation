# Detailed 9:16 visual prompts

1. Hook: macro cinematic view of a hand hovering near the opposite ribs, warm skin tones, deep navy background, shallow depth of field.
2. Contrast: split composition showing self-touch and an external hand, matched fingertip paths, distinct cyan and amber arrows.
3. Motor prediction: simplified brain with a motor command branching into movement and a prediction copy; label “efference copy”, not a literal wire.
4. Cerebellum: stylized brain with posterior-inferior cerebellum highlighted; show predicted touch location and timing.
5. Sensory attenuation: paired waveforms where predicted self-touch produces a smaller response; never show a zero signal or hard off-switch.
6. Human evidence: cerebellum and somatosensory cortical regions connected; label “2020 human fMRI study”; use an association line, not a causal arrow.
7. Payoff: external touch arrives without the person's motor prediction and is less attenuated; finish “Your brain predicted your own touch.”

## Motion and layout
- 1080×1920, 9:16, 30 fps.
- Slow 3–5% push-ins; no flashing or strobing.
- Captions above bottom 15% safe zone.
- Do not portray the cerebellum as a single switch that turns touch off.
- Explain this as a major sensory-prediction component, not the complete theory of every kind of tickle.
