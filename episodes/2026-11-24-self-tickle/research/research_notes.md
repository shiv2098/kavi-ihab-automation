# Research brief — 24 November 2026

## Core mechanism
Self-generated touch is usually perceived as less intense and less ticklish than externally generated touch. A leading account is predictive sensory attenuation: when the motor system issues a movement command, a copy of that command (often called an efference copy) is used by internal forward models to predict the sensory consequences. When the predicted touch arrives in the expected place and time, responses in somatosensory processing areas are attenuated. The cerebellum is strongly implicated, but it is not accurate to describe it as a single switch that turns touch off.

## Primary evidence
1. Blakemore, Wolpert & Frith (1998), Nature Neuroscience, “Central cancellation of self-produced tickle sensation.” fMRI showed more somatosensory activity for externally produced touch; the authors proposed cerebellar prediction of self-generated touch. https://doi.org/10.1038/2870
2. Kilteni & Ehrsson (2020), Journal of Neuroscience. In healthy human participants, secondary somatosensory responses were attenuated during self-generated touch. Stronger cerebellum–somatosensory connectivity correlated with stronger behavioral attenuation. Open access CC BY 4.0: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6975290/
3. Knoetsch & Zimmermann (2021), Consciousness and Cognition. Demonstrated spatially specific attenuation and noted predictability is part of the explanation, not necessarily the only factor. Open access CC BY 4.0: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8193814/
4. eLife (2019), “Rapid learning and unlearning of predicted sensory delays in self-generated touch.” Delaying feedback can change ticklishness, and predictions can adapt over repeated trials. https://elifesciences.org/articles/42888

## Fact-check
- Say “usually feels weaker”, not “impossible”; self-generated touch can still be felt.
- Say “one major reason”; tickling also includes social, affective and contextual components.
- Sensory attenuation reduces perceived intensity; it does not erase the signal.
- The 2020 study found a correlation between connectivity and attenuation; do not turn that into proof of a single causal pathway.
