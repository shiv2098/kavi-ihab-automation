## Description
Try to tickle your own ribs and the sensation usually feels weaker than when someone else does it. A leading explanation is predictive sensory attenuation: your motor system sends a copy of its movement command to prediction circuits, including the cerebellum. When the expected touch arrives, activity in touch-processing areas is reduced.

A 2020 human brain-imaging study linked stronger cerebellum–somatosensory connectivity with stronger attenuation of self-generated touch. This is a major part of the explanation, not a complete theory of every kind of tickling.

Research:
- Blakemore, Wolpert & Frith (1998): https://doi.org/10.1038/2870
- Kilteni & Ehrsson (2020): https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6975290/
- Knoetsch & Zimmermann (2021): https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8193814/
