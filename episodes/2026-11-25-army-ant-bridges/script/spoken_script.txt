How can a bridge move while hundreds of ants are walking over it? Some army ants build living roadways by linking their bodies across gaps in the forest floor. Their nestmates stream over the structure instead of following a longer detour. But the bridge keeps changing. In field experiments with Eciton hamatum, researchers watched bridges lengthen, widen and shift toward the main trail. The movement increased when traffic was heavier. The likely mechanism is local: workers in busier sections are more likely to be joined by passing ants, while less-contacted workers are more likely to leave. No ant needs to know the whole route. The shortcut saves travel, but every ant holding the bridge is temporarily unavailable for foraging. So the structure settles where the colony's traffic benefit is balanced against the workers it ties up.
