## Description
Some Eciton army ants link their bodies to bridge gaps along their foraging trails. Their nestmates use the living structure as a shortcut. Field experiments found that bridges can lengthen, widen and shift as traffic changes.

The likely local mechanism is simple: workers in busier parts are more likely to be joined by passing ants, while less-contacted workers are more likely to leave. Researchers interpret the changing bridge as a group-level trade-off between shorter travel and workers temporarily tied up as supports. No individual ant needs to know the whole route.

Research:
- Reid et al. (2015), PNAS: https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1512241112
