# Research brief — 25 November 2026

## Primary study
Reid, C. R., Lutz, M. J., Powell, S., Kao, A. B., Couzin, I. D., and Garnier, S. (2015). “Army ants dynamically adjust living bridges in response to a cost–benefit trade-off.” Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences, 112(49), 15113–15118. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1512241112

The field experiments focused on Eciton hamatum in Panama. Workers link their bodies to span gaps along foraging trails; nestmates use the structure as a living roadway. The paper reports bridges ranging from a few individuals to many hundreds, depending on the gap and terrain.

In experimental conditions, bridges lengthened, widened and changed position. They moved toward the main trail axis, creating shortcuts. Greater traffic was associated with greater bridge movement. Movement plateaued before the maximum possible shortcut.

## Measured vs inferred
Measured: bridge position and dimensions changed with time, traffic intensity and experimental gap geometry.

Authors' proposed local mechanism: individuals in higher-contact parts of a bridge are more likely to be joined by passing workers, while individuals in lower-contact regions are more likely to leave. Local joining and leaving can move the structure without any individual knowing the global route or colony-wide cost/benefit.

Model interpretation: a cost–benefit model qualitatively matched observed bridge movement. The authors explicitly caution that their data do not prove the observed stopping positions are exactly the mathematical optimum.

## Fact-check guardrails
- Identify the studied species as Eciton hamatum; do not generalize every detail to every army-ant species.
- “Hundreds” describes a possible bridge size, not every bridge.
- Say workers link their bodies; do not invent a specific anatomical clasp.
- Bridges can lengthen, widen and shift; they are not static ropes.
- The group-level trade-off is an interpretation supported by observations and a model, not conscious planning.
- Workers in the bridge are temporarily unavailable for other foraging tasks; do not invent a percentage of the colony committed.
