Why does a popcorn kernel jump after it bursts? The answer is hiding in its own starch. Inside each kernel, a little water sits behind a tough outer coat called the pericarp. Heating builds pressure while the starch softens. Near 180 degrees Celsius, the shell ruptures and the hot starch rushes out. High-speed cameras, filming at 2,900 frames per second, revealed a thin tongue of starch pressing against the pan. It bends like a spring, then rebounds and launches the popcorn. And the familiar pop? Researchers synchronized sound with the footage and found it happens during the release of water vapour, before the jump. So the sound and the leap have different causes: escaping vapour likely makes the pop; a tiny starch spring makes the jump.
