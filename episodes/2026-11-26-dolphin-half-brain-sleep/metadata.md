Description:
Dolphins are voluntary breathers, so they must surface for air. Their unusual sleep pattern—unihemispheric slow-wave sleep—lets one brain hemisphere show slow-wave sleep while the other remains relatively activated. In studied dolphins and belugas, the eye opposite the sleeping hemisphere is often closed, while the other eye is more often open. But eye position is only a clue, not a perfect brain-state detector. The two hemispheres can take turns resting.
