# Research brief — 20 November 2026

## Episode
How do bats avoid collisions when thousands emerge from a crowded cave?

## 2025 field study
Goldshtein et al., PNAS (2025), studied greater mouse-tailed bats (Rhinopoma microphyllum) in Israel's Hula Valley. Researchers tracked dozens of bats within a much larger emerging group and mounted miniature ultrasonic microphones on some animals to record the acoustic scene from a bat's point of view.

The paper reports severe acoustic masking by other bats' calls, declining within seconds as bats spread out. The Max Planck research summary reports that, within about five seconds after emergence, bats fanned out and changed to shorter, weaker calls at higher frequencies.

Methodological caveat: tagged bats were released outside the cave; direct recordings at the most crowded cave opening were missing. A computational model reconstructed the densest emergence stage. Do not describe the “94% jammed” estimate as a direct microphone measurement at the cave mouth. The script omits that number.

## 2026 model
Mazar & Yovel, eLife (Version of Record, 2 March 2026), developed a two-dimensional sensorimotor/agent-based model of bats exiting a crowded roost. It suggests repeated calls with short intervals, short-term integration across multiple calls, and simple movement rules (follow walls, identify gaps, avoid nearby obstacles/conspecifics) can support navigation despite interference.

This is a model, not a direct recording of each bat's internal decision process. Its assumptions include distinguishing wall echoes from conspecific echoes and a simplified 2D environment. Frame its results as a plausible strategy, not proof of every real bat's exact behavior.

## Fact-check guardrails
- Echolocation uses emitted high-frequency calls and returning echoes.
- The field species was greater mouse-tailed bat, Rhinopoma microphyllum, in Hula Valley, Israel.
- Do not claim bats never collide or that all species use identical calls.
- Do not state that 94% was directly measured at the cave mouth.
- Do not present the 2026 model's wall-following/gap-finding sequence as proven real-bat behavior.
