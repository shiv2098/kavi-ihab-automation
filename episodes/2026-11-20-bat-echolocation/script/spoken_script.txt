How do bats avoid crashing when thousands pour out of one cave? They use echolocation: short ultrasonic calls bounce off walls and nearby bodies, then return as echoes. But in a crowded exit, other bats' calls can mask those echoes. In a 2025 study, researchers tracked greater mouse-tailed bats and recorded sound from microphones mounted on some animals. They found intense acoustic masking during emergence; it dropped within seconds as the bats spread out. The bats also shifted to shorter, weaker, higher-frequency calls. A 2026 computer model suggests another tool: combine information from several calls, follow walls, and steer through gaps while avoiding nearby bats. That model tests a possible strategy—it does not prove every bat uses exactly those steps. Their trick is to keep sensing, adjust, and move.
